# Journal practice

Journal practice is optional, private, and durable only by deliberate choice.
Focus distinguishes a momentary Transition Check-In, a Reflection, a Journal,
and a Journal Entry so that ordinary context shifts do not become a permanent
life log.

## Distinctions

| Concept | Purpose | Default duration | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transition Check-In | Preserve what is happening now and the next chosen step at a context shift | Ephemeral or short-lived | Not a Journal Entry and not automatically authoritative |
| Reflection | Consider an outcome, friction, correction, or question | May remain on its original surface | Becomes durable only if the individual chooses |
| Journal | Hold selected private Reflections durably | Defined by the individual | A record of authority only when designated for Journal Entries |
| Journal Entry | Preserve a deliberately admitted Reflection | Durable under the Journal's policy | Controlled by the designated Journal for that purpose |

A Transition Check-In becomes a Journal Entry only through explicit promotion
into a Journal the individual has designated as the relevant record of
authority. Age, sentiment, repeated access, or AI analysis cannot perform that
promotion.

## Transition Check-Ins

A Check-In may record:

- what is happening now;
- the next chosen step;
- a material constraint or stopping point; and
- whether it should expire after the transition.

It does not require a date, mood, score, category, streak, template, daily
cadence, or permanent retention. It may end without action. Its subject matter
does not automatically make it useful for later Reflection.

## Reflection

Reflection supports Learning. It may ask:

- What reduced restart effort or confusion?
- What created review, correction, or cognitive burden?
- What authority or privacy boundary was unclear?
- What should be corrected, simplified, retained, released, or stopped?
- What open question is worth carrying forward?

Reflection may remain in a paper margin, a temporary digital note, or memory. A
person does not need a Journal to Reflect.

## Designating a Journal

When the individual chooses a Journal, state:

- the surface or collection designated as the record of authority for Journal
  Entries;
- the life scope it may contain;
- what may be admitted and what must remain elsewhere;
- retention, correction, deletion, and portable-exit policy;
- who or what may access it;
- whether any selected entry may be resurfaced in a Focus Review or Personal
  Mission Control; and
- whether AI assistance is allowed, and only under what entry-specific
  admission.

A Journal designation is purpose-specific. It does not make the Journal
authoritative for tasks, appointments, shared commitments, external records, or
the broader Focus practice.

## Admitting a Reflection

Before admission, the individual decides:

1. Which exact Reflection is useful to preserve?
2. Why should it be durable?
3. Does it include private, third-party, work-confidential, or sensitive
   material that should remain on the source surface or be narrowed?
4. What source and corrections should remain linked?
5. Which Journal is authoritative for this entry?
6. What retention, resurfacing, and AI-access rules apply?

Admission records the source, selected content, Journal, and visible result. It
does not convert every related Check-In, source, or private history into Journal
content. When the Reflection began as a Transition Check-In, admission into the
Journal is an explicit promotion; no Check-In becomes a Journal Entry
automatically.

## Selective resurfacing

A Journal Entry appears in a Focus Review or Personal Mission Control only when
the individual deliberately selects it for a current purpose. Resurfacing
should reveal:

- why the entry is relevant now;
- its source and age;
- whether it is a direct record or a projection;
- what portion is being exposed; and
- how to dismiss or remove it from the current context.

The absence of a recent entry is not a problem to fix. Focus does not require
daily journaling, sentiment tracking, trend analysis, streaks, or prompts that
penalize silence.

## Optional AI assistance

AI may assist with one or more explicitly selected Journal Entries when the
individual chooses. It may propose themes, questions, contradictions, or a
draft summary that cites the selected entry or source span.

AI must not:

- receive blanket Journal access by default;
- infer permission from account access;
- treat a Journal Entry as an instruction to act;
- diagnose the individual or make medical or therapeutic claims;
- silently create, edit, promote, share, or delete Journal Entries;
- use an unrelated entry because it appears relevant; or
- expose Journal material in a shared promotion without exact selection and
  preview.

The individual reviews every proposal and decides whether it is useful. The
complete Journal practice remains available without AI.

## Shared boundaries

Journal content is private even when it concerns work or another person. If a
bounded insight would help a shared external record, create a separate artifact
that includes only the selected material needed for that purpose. Preview it,
confirm consequential details, and promote it deliberately. Do not expose the
originating Journal Entry or surrounding private history unless the individual
explicitly chooses and has appropriate authority.

## Correction, deletion, and exit

The individual must be able to:

- correct an entry while preserving necessary provenance;
- distinguish a correction from rewriting the historical source;
- remove an entry or projection according to policy;
- disconnect AI or another supporting tool;
- recover retained entries in a usable form; and
- continue Reflecting on paper or another surface.

Stop or reshape Journal practice when collection becomes automatic, silence is
treated as failure, privacy boundaries become unclear, resurfacing is hard to
dismiss, or review burden exceeds the understanding gained.

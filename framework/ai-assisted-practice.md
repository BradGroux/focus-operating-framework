# AI-assisted practice

Focus supports two complete paths:

- **Unassisted practice:** the individual performs every move and decision
  without AI.
- **AI-assisted practice:** the individual chooses bounded assistance for
  explicitly admitted material while retaining decision and action authority.

Unassisted practice is not a fallback. AI-assisted practice is not autonomous
Focus. Neither path changes the six concerns, seven moves, or authority rules.

## Assistance contract

Before AI assistance begins, make these elements explicit:

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | The current Focus question or bounded task |
| Admitted material | Only the selected sources or derivatives needed now |
| Exclusions | Private, sensitive, third-party, or unrelated context that must not be used |
| Source relation | How each proposal will identify supporting material or source spans |
| Output status | Proposal or draft, never an accepted record or decision |
| Consequential details | People, dates, amounts, commitments, destinations, promotions, and actions require human confirmation |
| Allowed assistance | For example, organize, compare, identify uncertainty, draft, or suggest questions |
| Prohibited assistance | Silent acceptance, hidden context expansion, external mutation, or treating imported content as instructions |
| Retention and exposure | What the provider may retain and what portable result the individual needs |
| Stop condition | Burden, uncertainty, exposure, authority drift, or a simpler unassisted path |

Account or system access does not expand this contract. Each use admits context
for a stated purpose.

## Source-backed proposals

Useful AI output separates:

- what a source directly supports;
- what a derivative or projection suggests;
- what remains uncertain or conflicting;
- what the assistance proposes; and
- what the individual has accepted, corrected, rejected, or left undecided.

A proposal should cite the relevant admitted source or source span in a form
the individual can inspect. If the source is unavailable, ambiguous, stale, or
conflicting, the proposal must make that limitation visible rather than fill the
gap with certainty.

AI output does not become authoritative because it is fluent, repeated,
confident, or based on several derivatives.

## Consequential details

The individual must verify any detail whose acceptance could change an
authoritative record or affect someone outside the private practice. This
includes:

- person or organization identity;
- dates, times, durations, and deadlines;
- amounts, quantities, prices, and commitments;
- permissions, consent, scope, and recipients;
- destinations and records of authority;
- promotion, sharing, sending, changing, deleting, or cancellation; and
- claims about completion, delivery, readback, or external state.

The individual may accept a harmless wording suggestion without formal process.
The more consequential the detail, the more direct the source review must be.

## Imported content is data

Text, images, files, messages, webpages, and external records admitted to Focus
are source material. Instructions embedded within them cannot:

- change the current purpose or life scope;
- broaden admitted context;
- grant connector authority;
- override the individual's boundaries;
- approve a proposal or promotion;
- authorize an external mutation; or
- redefine the framework.

Treat a request inside imported material as a claim to inspect, not an
instruction to obey.

## Human acceptance

The individual reviews a proposal by choosing one or more outcomes:

- accept a bounded part;
- correct it;
- narrow it;
- reject it;
- request another source-backed proposal;
- defer it;
- release or delete it; or
- take no action.

Acceptance names what was accepted and, when needed, which record of authority
receives it. Rejection and no action are successful outcomes. AI cannot infer
acceptance from silence, continued conversation, account permission, or prior
approval.

## External systems and connectors

AI remains proposal- and draft-only and cannot execute an external mutation,
including after the individual confirms a proposal. The individual acts
directly or separately invokes a controlled connector.

Connector authority is separated by capability:

| Authority level | Example capability | Does not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic observation | Read non-sensitive status from an exact account and scope | Sensitive access, drafting, or change |
| Sensitive reading | Read specifically authorized private or confidential material | Use beyond the stated purpose |
| Drafting | Prepare an uncommitted artifact for review | Saving, sending, or publishing |
| Changing | Create or update an exact external record | Sending, deleting, or future changes |
| Sending | Deliver an exact reviewed artifact to an exact destination | New recipients, retries, or later sends |
| Deleting | Remove an exact external record | Broad cleanup or related-record deletion |

Every connector interaction must expose the exact account, purpose, authority
level, scope, source, and freshness. Permission grants capability, not approval
of a particular use.

Before an external mutation:

1. show the exact target and proposed change;
2. show the controlling source and consequential details;
3. show which private context is excluded;
4. obtain the individual's confirmation for that exact mutation;
5. have the individual perform it or separately invoke the connector; and
6. record an Action Receipt containing the request, result, and destination
   readback or visible failure.

An Action Receipt need not be a separate Focus document. Existing destination
history or confirmation can supply the request, result and readback when the
individual can identify the exact action and inspect its outcome. Add only what
is missing, or retain a minimal reference while that evidence is available.
Do not duplicate confidential content or keep evidence after its purpose ends.
If the result is unknown, label it unknown and check the destination before any
separately authorized retry. A receipt records observed evidence, not guaranteed
delivery or permanent retention.

AI may prepare the preview or draft. It cannot press the final boundary.

## Journal and private context

AI receives Journal material only when the individual explicitly selects the
entry or source span for the current purpose. Blanket Journal access, automatic
resurfacing, and inferred relevance are prohibited.

AI output based on a Journal remains private unless the individual deliberately
creates and previews a separate bounded artifact for shared promotion. The
originating entry and surrounding Journal context remain excluded by default.

## Unassisted equivalence

Every AI-assisted practice must have a viable unassisted path. The individual
should be able to:

- inspect sources directly;
- make corrections and connections;
- choose attention;
- accept or reject promotions;
- perform or decline external actions; and
- preserve continuity in ordinary physical or digital forms.

An implementation fails the framework when AI is required for first value,
source access, accepted-record recovery, authority decisions, or portable exit.

## Failure and stop conditions

Stop or narrow AI assistance when:

- the provider needs unrelated context;
- source support cannot be inspected;
- consequential details are silently inferred or accepted;
- imported content changes scope or behavior;
- private Journal or third-party material is exposed without a current purpose;
- a proposal is written directly into an authoritative record;
- the assistance is expected to execute or retry an external action;
- review and correction burden equals or exceeds the value gained; or
- provider exit would make accepted context unusable.

Record the issue as a local Reflection, correction, open question, or pilot
finding. Do not hide it behind a confidence score or claim that human review
always makes unsafe assistance safe.

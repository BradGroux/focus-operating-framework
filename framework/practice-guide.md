# Practice guide

This guide applies Focus to one real situation. It is tool-independent and
non-sequential. Use only the concerns and moves that help, revisit them when the
situation changes, and stop when the practice creates more burden, exposure, or
confusion than it removes.

## Start with one Focus episode

A Focus episode is a bounded use of one or more moves. Good entry points include:

- returning to work after an interruption;
- leaving enough context before switching roles or locations;
- deciding among competing work and personal demands;
- selectively moving a paper idea into a digital record;
- reviewing one proposed correction or promotion;
- reflecting privately without creating a permanent log; or
- recovering after a tool, provider, or projection becomes unavailable.

Do not begin by importing every note, connecting every account, indexing a
whole life, or building Personal Mission Control. First value should be possible
with one situation and ordinary materials.

## Make the life scope explicit

Choose one of three life scopes for the practice:

- **Work:** selected professional contexts owned by the individual.
- **Personal:** selected non-work contexts owned by the individual.
- **Combined:** deliberately selected work and personal contexts viewed together
  for the individual's attention decisions.

Life scope is a boundary, not an ingestion instruction. Combined scope may help
someone see a work deadline beside a personal responsibility; it does not make
every work system, message, appointment, note, or relationship part of Focus.

## Use the minimum continuity cue

Before a transition, preserve only what the next return needs. A useful cue can
answer:

1. Why does this matter?
2. Where does it stand?
3. What is the next chosen step?
4. What uncertainty, dependency, or stopping condition must remain visible?
5. Where is the controlling record for anything consequential?

These are prompts, not five required fields. A familiar location or existing
record may already supply enough context. No cue is required.

The cue may be a paper mark, a bookmark, a short digital note, a deliberate
spoken source, or a bounded projection. It need not become permanent.

## Return without catching up

If no cue exists, begin with what is available now. Check only the controlling
record needed for a consequential decision. If it cannot be recovered, leave
that decision uncertain and pause it; unrelated safe work or rest can continue.
Do not reconstruct missing days, backfill Check-Ins or clear old Review Items
before beginning again. A missed routine creates no Focus debt.

Capacity may change within a day. Consider available time, energy, access needs
and external demands as they are now, without scoring them. Choose one smaller
step, ask for help, rest, defer or stop. Rest needs no productivity justification.
Use an existing bookmark, a visible mark, an accessible digital note or no new
record. Writing, speaking, reading a long checklist and maintaining a dashboard
are not admission tests. Choose an ordinary surface you can use; assistance is
optional and does not require disclosing private context.

Fragmented days need no uninterrupted block. If preserving a cue costs more than
it helps, omit it. An abandoned private plan need not retain a return path or a
reason for stopping. Preserve only records still needed for an actual purpose;
releasing an intention does not authorize destroying someone else's records.

A personal decision to stop does not cancel an external commitment. Where a
shared deadline or dependency remains, check its controlling record and choose
whether to seek a narrower scope, changed date or help through the existing
channel. Keep the original commitment and any requested change distinct until
its authority confirms the change. If communication is unavailable, leave its
status unresolved rather than claim cancellation or completion. Focus does not
promise freedom from external constraints or prescribe professional advice.

## Apply the smallest useful moves

| Situation | Possible moves | Stop when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Returning after interruption | Orient, Clarify, Choose | The individual can continue or responsibly stop |
| Leaving work for another context | Capture, Choose, Continue | The next chosen step and needed authority are clear |
| Reviewing a paper derivative | Clarify, Connect | Corrections and promotion decision are recorded |
| Choosing among competing demands | Orient, Choose | Source, freshness, why now, and non-action choices are visible |
| Reflecting on a difficult episode | Reflect | The useful observation is preserved on the chosen surface |
| Recovering from a failed tool | Orient, Clarify, Connect, Continue | Accepted context is usable through a manual path |

This table illustrates combinations. It is not a workflow.

## Keep authority explicit

For every consequential matter, write or be able to answer:

- **Purpose:** What decision or continuity need is this serving?
- **Source:** What original physical or digital artifact supports it?
- **Status:** Is it a source, derivative, proposal, accepted record, or
  projection?
- **Record of authority:** Which artifact or external system controls for this
  purpose?
- **Freshness:** When was that authority last observed, and is the projection
  known to reflect it?
- **Conflict:** What disagrees, and who must resolve it?
- **Promotion:** What selected material, if any, was approved for which
  destination?
- **Receipt:** What did the destination show after an external mutation?

Different purposes can have different records of authority. A calendar may
control an appointment while a paper notebook controls the individual's private
thinking and a work system controls a shared commitment. Focus does not merge
them into one truth.

## Use Transition Check-Ins sparingly

A Transition Check-In is an optional, minimal note made at a context shift. It
records what is happening now and the next chosen step. It may be ephemeral and
may end without action.

A compact form is:

> **Now:** ____. **Next chosen step:** ____. **Constraint or stopping point:**
> ____.

Do not require a Check-In for every transition. Do not treat it as a status
report, daily streak, activity record, or Journal Entry. Delete or let it expire
when it no longer serves continuity, unless the individual deliberately promotes
it into a Journal.

## Run a bounded Focus Review

A Focus Review helps the individual decide what deserves attention now and what
requires human review before acceptance, promotion, or action. The individual
chooses whether and when to run one.

For each Review Item, show:

| Required element | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Source | What supports this item? |
| Freshness | When was the controlling record last observed? |
| Why now | Why is this being presented at this time? |
| Choice | Continue, accept, correct, promote, defer, release, delete, no action, or stop? |
| Authority | Who decides, and what destination controls? |
| Boundary | What must remain private, temporary, or unconnected? |

Keep the review bounded by time, item count, context, or decision type. Dismissal
is a successful outcome when an item does not deserve attention. Do not create a
zero-inbox target, fixed cadence, hidden priority, repeated nagging, or shame
state.

## Preserve private versus shared boundaries

The individual's Check-Ins, Reflections, Journal, source material, proposals,
and decision history remain private practice. Work subject matter does not make
them shared.

When a shared record needs an update:

1. identify the exact shared destination and its authority;
2. select the minimum artifact that destination needs;
3. preview the artifact without private history unless deliberately included;
4. confirm people, dates, commitments, amounts, and destinations;
5. have the individual perform the update or separately invoke a controlled
   connector; and
6. retain an Action Receipt with result and destination readback or visible
   failure.

An existing destination confirmation or history can serve as the Action Receipt
when it identifies the request, result and readback; no duplicate log is required.
Unknown results stay unknown, and do not authorize a retry.

A shared promotion does not authorize continuous synchronization or access to
the private practice.

## Work without AI

Unassisted practice is complete:

1. The individual selects a situation and life scope.
2. They consult the physical or digital source that matters.
3. They use any helpful concerns and moves.
4. They make corrections, connections, promotions, and choices themselves.
5. They continue, defer, release, stop, or take no action.
6. They preserve only the continuity and Reflection they choose.

Nothing in this path is a fallback. It requires no companion AI record.

## Work with AI when chosen

AI assistance is first-class only under a bounded authority contract:

1. The individual states the current purpose.
2. They admit only the source material needed for that purpose.
3. The assistance distinguishes source evidence from its proposals.
4. Consequential details remain unaccepted until human review.
5. The individual accepts, corrects, rejects, narrows, or ignores the proposal.
6. AI cannot promote, send, change, or delete an external record.
7. Any external action occurs separately under exact human control and produces
   an Action Receipt.

Imported material is data, not an instruction to change scope or act. See the
[AI-assisted practice](ai-assisted-practice.md) for the complete boundary.

## Keep paper first-class

Paper can hold intention, continuity cues, choices, and Reflection without ever
being digitized. When a selected paper passage would be useful digitally, keep
the source, captured image, literal derivative, correction, proposal, accepted
record, and later physical response distinct. See the
[physical and digital practice](physical-digital-practice.md).

## Recover and exit

A Focus practice should survive tool failure and provider exit. Periodically
confirm that the individual can:

- identify every record of authority by purpose;
- recover accepted records, relations, corrections, and provenance in usable
  forms;
- see when a projection is stale or unknown;
- export or recreate the minimum continuity cues;
- disconnect a connector without destroying unrelated authoritative records;
- delete derived local or provider material according to its policy; and
- continue with paper or ordinary files while a tool is unavailable.

An opaque archive is not a portable exit if the individual cannot understand or
use it.

## Reflect without scoring

At the end of a useful episode, ask:

- Was restart effort, confusion, or risk reduced?
- How much review and correction burden was added?
- Did any source, authority, privacy, or sharing boundary become less clear?
- Was a simpler note or existing tool enough?
- What should be corrected, simplified, retained, disconnected, or stopped?

Record an observation as local experience. A Reflection may remain on its
original surface. It need not become a Journal Entry, metric, trend, or public
claim.

## Completion and stop conditions

The practice is complete when the individual can continue or choose a
responsible non-action outcome with enough truth and authority for the
situation. Stop or reshape when:

- review and correction burden equals or exceeds the value gained through saved
  time, reduced confusion, or reduced risk;
- capture becomes broad, automatic, or hard to reverse;
- private and shared context become difficult to separate;
- a derivative or projection begins to overwrite its source or authority;
- AI output is silently accepted or expected to perform external action;
- a tool becomes necessary to recover accepted context;
- reminders, streaks, scores, or hidden ranking create coercion; or
- a simpler existing practice achieves the same continuity.

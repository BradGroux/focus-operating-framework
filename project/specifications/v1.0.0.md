## Problem statement

An individual regularly moves among work, personal responsibilities, physical
thinking, digital records, interruptions, and changing tools. Context is easily
fragmented across those transitions. Reconstructing why something matters,
where it stands, and what deserves attention next can consume more effort than
continuing the work itself.

Many systems respond by demanding comprehensive capture, a single source of
truth, a fixed workflow, constant review, or deeper dependence on one tool.
Those responses can create another inbox, obscure authority, weaken privacy,
displace paper, and turn assistance into control.

Focus needs a small, coherent, tool-agnostic framework that helps one individual
preserve continuity and direct attention without requiring a particular
application, capture surface, life scope, provider, or automation model.

## Definition

The Focus Operating Framework is an open individual operating framework for
directing attention and preserving continuity across the work and personal
contexts a person chooses. It combines physical and digital practice, supports
complete unassisted and AI-assisted paths, and keeps the individual
authoritative.

## Mission

Help individuals carry intention and context through transitions, choose what
deserves attention, continue meaningful work, and learn without surrendering
privacy, records, or judgment to a tool.

## Audience and first job

Focus serves one individual. That individual may apply it to work life,
personal life, or both. Work-related material does not turn Focus into a team or
business operating framework, and a combined life scope does not authorize
whole-life capture.

The first job is:

> After an interruption or context switch, help an individual recover why the
> chosen work matters, where it stands, and the next chosen step with less
> restart effort.

The framework names this outcome **Focus continuity**. It does not require
uninterrupted attention or equate activity volume with success.

## Product solution

Produce a concise documentation framework that:

- defines six concerns that an individual must keep clear;
- defines seven flexible moves that may be entered, skipped, repeated, or
  stopped according to context;
- treats paper and digital tools as complementary practice surfaces;
- distinguishes sources, derivatives, proposals, accepted records,
  projections, and records of authority;
- makes selective admission, privacy, retention, provenance, correction,
  promotion, portable exit, and human authority explicit;
- defines complete unassisted and AI-assisted paths;
- provides optional Journal, Focus Review, and Personal Mission Control
  patterns;
- illustrates the framework through four focused examples and three complex
  case studies;
- includes a bounded owner-pilot protocol for optional evidence work; and
- publishes reusable review prompts and exact-candidate review records.

The framework remains usable without software. Any future application is a
non-authoritative example implementation that pins an exact Focus release.

## Core commitments

1. **The individual remains authoritative.** Tools and assistance never own
   attention, records, or actions.
2. **Attention is chosen, not assigned.** Rankings and reminders may inform but
   cannot decide what matters.
3. **Continuity matters more than activity volume.** Preserve enough context to
   return; do not reward constant capture or completion.
4. **Paper is first-class.** Physical practice remains valid without
   digitization, destruction, or proprietary materials.
5. **Admission is selective.** Bring in only what serves a stated purpose.
6. **Sources remain distinct from derivatives.** Correction and promotion never
   erase provenance.
7. **Every move is optional and contextual.** Deferring, releasing, stopping,
   and taking no action are legitimate.
8. **AI is optional, integral when chosen, and advisory.** Consequential
   interpretation and action remain human decisions.
9. **Tools remain replaceable.** Accepted records support portable exit and
   manual continuity.
10. **Learning must not become judgment.** Reflection improves the practice
    without streaks, shame, productivity scores, or universal outcome claims.

## Canonical concerns

The concerns describe what an individual must keep clear. They are not stages,
screens, departments, or scores.

1. **Intention** — why something matters, what the individual is choosing, and
   when stopping is valid.
2. **Attention** — what deserves focus now, why now, and what is deliberately
   deferred or released.
3. **Continuity** — what context must survive interruptions, transitions,
   physical and digital movement, and tool failure.
4. **Authority** — which source, record, and person controls; how proposals
   become accepted and actions authorized.
5. **Boundaries** — what may be admitted, retained, exposed, automated, or
   removed, including cognitive and privacy limits.
6. **Learning** — how reflection, correction, outcomes, and friction improve the
   individual's practice without scoring or shame.

## Canonical moves

The moves are flexible actions, not a mandatory lifecycle. An individual may
use one move, combine several, repeat them, remain paper-only, or stop without
reaching a prescribed completion state.

1. **Orient** — name the present context, intention, constraints, and valid
   stopping point.
2. **Capture** — selectively preserve a physical, digital, or spoken source with
   enough provenance.
3. **Clarify** — review derivatives, correct uncertainty, and separate source
   facts from proposals.
4. **Connect** — relate selected material to useful context or explicitly
   promote it into a record of authority.
5. **Choose** — decide what deserves attention now and what to defer, release,
   or leave unchanged.
6. **Continue** — resume or perform chosen work while leaving enough context for
   the next transition.
7. **Reflect** — examine outcomes and friction, correct records, and adapt the
   practice proportionately.

## Required framework behavior

### Individual authority and life scope

- One individual owns each Focus practice.
- The individual chooses work, personal, or combined life scope.
- The framework does not define team ownership, assignments, management views,
  shared workspaces, or organizational workflows.
- Private Focus context remains private even when its subject matter concerns
  shared work.
- A bounded artifact may enter a shared external record only through deliberate,
  previewed promotion.

### Selective admission and capture

- Capture is foreground, deliberate, optional, and surface-neutral.
- Paper, direct digital entry, and deliberate spoken capture may all illustrate
  the framework.
- Ambient recording, background collection, watched libraries, bulk ingestion,
  and capture-completeness goals are prohibited.
- Admitted material has a stated purpose and does not imply permanent retention
  or broader use.
- Sensitive or third-party material requires separate justification and
  appropriate authority or consent.

### Physical and digital practice

- Paper is a first-class thinking and reflection surface.
- Paper-only completion remains valid.
- Digitization is selective, purpose-specific, optional, and provenance-bearing.
- A captured image, literal derivative, cleaned representation, proposal,
  accepted record, physical prompt, and later physical response remain distinct.
- No capture authorizes destruction of a physical source or indefinite
  retention of a provider copy.
- The flagship example must demonstrate one complete physical-digital-physical
  round trip without making that round trip mandatory.

### Authority, provenance, and promotion

- The individual explicitly designates a record of authority for each purpose.
- Different purposes may use different records of authority.
- Focus never becomes a universal source of truth.
- Sources, derivatives, proposals, accepted records, and projections retain
  distinct identities.
- A derivative does not become authoritative merely by existing.
- Promotion requires human approval, a named destination, preserved provenance,
  and visible success or failure.
- Silent merge, silent overwrite, and latest-timestamp-wins are prohibited.
- Conflicts remain visible until accountable review resolves them.

### Transition Check-Ins and Journal

- A Transition Check-In is minimal, optional, and made at a context shift.
- It records what is happening now and the next chosen step.
- It may be ephemeral and may end without action.
- A Journal is an optional, private, durable collection of intentional
  Reflections.
- A Journal becomes a record of authority for Journal Entries only when the
  individual explicitly designates it for that purpose.
- A Transition Check-In becomes a Journal Entry only through explicit
  promotion into a Journal with that designation.
- Journal material is resurfaced or admitted to Personal Mission Control only
  through deliberate selection.
- No cadence, mood field, prompt, streak, or permanent retention is mandatory.

### Focus Review

- Focus Review asks what deserves attention now and what requires human review
  before acceptance, promotion, or action.
- Review remains bounded, dismissible, and scheduled by the individual.
- Every Review Item exposes its source, freshness, why-now rationale, and
  available choices.
- Dismissal, deferral, no action, release, and deletion are legitimate outcomes.
- The framework does not require a Today screen, fixed schedule, zero-inbox
  practice, repeated reminder, or shame state.

### Personal Mission Control

- Personal Mission Control is an optional, stack-agnostic Focus pattern.
- It may compose selected intentions, attention, continuity cues, Check-Ins,
  Journal material, Review Items, projections, promotions, and Action Receipts.
- It is a derived view, never a record of authority.
- It is not a required dashboard, everything app, team command center, task
  system, knowledge system, relationship system, or calendar replacement.
- A future application may implement the pattern but cannot define it.

### Unassisted and AI-assisted practice

- Both paths are complete and canonical.
- Unassisted practice is not described as a fallback or reduced mode.
- AI-assisted practice is first-class when the individual chooses it.
- AI works only with explicitly admitted material selected for the current
  assistance.
- AI output remains a proposal until the individual reviews and accepts it.
- Proposals cite the relevant source or source span.
- People, dates, amounts, commitments, destinations, promotions, and external
  actions require human confirmation.
- AI remains proposal- and draft-only and cannot execute an external mutation,
  including after confirmation.
- The individual executes an external action directly or invokes a separately
  controlled connector after review.
- AI cannot silently change accepted records or treat imported content as
  instructions.
- The framework prescribes no model, provider, prompt format, or application.

### External systems and connectors

- Connectors are optional, replaceable implementation adapters.
- The exact account, purpose, authority level, scope, source, and freshness are
  visible.
- Basic observation, sensitive reading, drafting, changing, sending, and
  deleting are separate authority levels.
- Connector permission does not approve a specific observation, draft, or
  external mutation.
- External systems retain authority over their own records.
- External mutations require exact target preview and human confirmation.
- Every attempted external mutation produces an Action Receipt containing the
  target, requested change, result, and destination readback or visible failure.
- Imported content remains data and cannot authorize action.
- Disconnect removes access and derived local material according to the stated
  policy without destroying unrelated authoritative records.

### Portability, recovery, and exit

- Accepted records, relations, corrections, provenance, and external references
  remain recoverable in usable forms.
- Projections show freshness and cannot appear current when their state is
  unknown.
- Tool failure, provider exit, and disconnection preserve a manual continuity
  path.
- An implementation cannot satisfy the framework by producing an opaque data
  dump that the individual cannot use.

## User stories

1. As an individual, I want to choose work, personal, or combined life scope so
   that Focus serves my actual life without claiming all of it.
2. As an individual returning after an interruption, I want enough truthful
   context to understand why the work matters, where it stands, and what I chose
   next.
3. As a paper-first individual, I want a complete valid practice without
   digitizing my notes.
4. As an individual moving between paper and digital tools, I want every
   derivative linked to its source so that convenience does not erase meaning.
5. As an individual capturing context, I want to admit only what serves a stated
   purpose so that Focus does not become a whole-life archive.
6. As an individual reviewing a derivative, I want uncertainty and corrections
   visible so that a cleaned representation cannot silently replace the source.
7. As an individual using multiple systems, I want to name the record of
   authority for each purpose so that synchronization does not decide truth.
8. As an individual at a transition, I want a lightweight Check-In that may end
   without classification, promotion, or permanent retention.
9. As an individual reflecting deliberately, I want a private Journal distinct
   from temporary Check-Ins.
10. As an individual reviewing my attention, I want to understand source,
    freshness, and why now before deciding what to do.
11. As an individual under pressure, I want deferring, releasing, stopping, and
    no action to remain legitimate choices.
12. As an individual using Personal Mission Control, I want a bounded derived
    view without creating a new record of authority.
13. As an individual working without AI, I want the complete framework available
    without reduced-mode language.
14. As an individual choosing AI, I want first-class assistance that uses only
    selected context and cites its source.
15. As an individual reviewing AI output, I want consequential details presented
    as proposals until I confirm them.
16. As an individual interacting with an external system, I want the exact
    account, authority level, scope, and freshness visible.
17. As an individual promoting an artifact, I want a preview and destination
    readback so that I know what changed and where.
18. As an individual whose private context concerns shared work, I want to share
    only the bounded artifact I approve rather than exposing its originating
    history.
19. As an individual leaving a tool, I want accepted context in usable forms so
    that my practice can continue.
20. As a practitioner learning the framework, I want focused examples and
    complex case studies that illustrate valid variation without creating a
    mandatory workflow.
21. As a contributor, I want the authority chain, language, boundaries, and
    evidence expectations clear before proposing a change.
22. As an independent reviewer, I want a bounded, tool-neutral prompt and exact
    candidate commit so that my findings are reproducible and publication-safe.
23. As the steward, I want unresolved findings and dissent preserved in a review
    disposition so that release approval is accountable.

## Required examples

All examples are original, fictional, tool-neutral, and explicitly
non-authoritative. Each states its purpose, life scope, assumptions, provenance,
limits, applicable concerns, moves used, authority decisions, boundaries,
outcome, and lessons. No example claims general effectiveness.

### Focused practice examples

1. **Paper-only personal reset** — a Transition Check-In, a chosen next step,
   and valid completion without digitization.
2. **Digital-only work resumption** — continuity through an interruption without
   creating another task system.
3. **Physical-digital-physical round trip** — selective source capture, literal
   derivative, human correction, explicit connection or promotion, minimal
   physical prompt, and linked handwritten response.
4. **Private Journal reflection with optional AI** — assistance with one selected
   Journal Entry without blanket Journal access or required promotion.

### Complex case studies

5. **Personal Mission Control across a mixed week** — work and personal
   intentions, Check-Ins, selected Journal material, external projections,
   Focus Review, AI proposals, bounded shared promotion, and Action Receipt.
6. **Long-running creative or research initiative** — paper ideation, digital
   research, interruptions, source-backed AI assistance, authoritative
   decisions, physical prompts, and reflection.
7. **Disruption and recovery** — competing work and personal changes, stale
   projections, conflicts, deferral, release, manual continuity, provider exit,
   and reflective reset.

## Owner pilot

The owner pilot is an optional evidence track introduced with v1.0.0. It tests
the framework with existing physical and digital practices; no Focus
application is required. Completion is not a prerequisite for the initial
documentation release, and a blank or incomplete protocol supports no outcome
claim.

### Minimum coverage

- Three weeks of real use by the founding steward.
- At least 30 representative Focus episodes.
- Work, personal, and combined life contexts.
- Paper-only, digital-only, physical-digital, unassisted, and AI-assisted paths.
- At least three Focus Reviews.
- Multiple Journal Entries and Transition Check-Ins.
- At least one complete physical-digital-physical round trip.
- Recorded restart effort, review and correction burden, usefulness, privacy
  friction, and authority confusion.

### Pilot pass criteria

- Every counted episode records a usefulness rating and net-burden comparison;
  an episode with missing required measures does not count toward the minimum
  30.
- The usefulness scale is: 1 — made the situation materially worse; 2 — burden
  exceeded value; 3 — mixed or neutral; 4 — clear net help; 5 — substantial net
  help.
- Median episode usefulness is at least 4 out of 5.
- Each episode records whether review and correction burden was lower than,
  equal to, or higher than the time, confusion, or risk removed.
- More than half of counted episodes record lower net burden, and fewer than
  three representative episodes record equal or higher net burden.
- Every admitted source and accepted promotion retains recoverable provenance
  and authority.
- No material privacy, accidental-sharing, silent-overwrite, or unauthorized-
  action failure occurs.
- All five required practice paths remain viable.
- The individual can defer, release, disconnect tools, and continue manually
  without losing accepted context.

### Pilot reshape or stop criteria

- The third representative episode with equal or higher net burden is recorded,
  whether or not those episodes are consecutive.
- A simpler note or existing tool provides the same result.
- Useful practice requires capture-everything, mandatory AI, mandatory
  connectors, hidden automation, or an everything app.
- Private versus shared or source versus derivative versus authority remains
  confusing.
- The method creates persistent review burden, coercive attention, or false
  completion pressure.

Pilot results are single-practitioner experience. They must not be presented as
market validation, clinical evidence, or proof of general effectiveness.
Completed results and any resulting framework changes are published through a
later governed version.

## Review and verification model

### Reviewer isolation

Each independent reviewer receives only:

- a clean, exact content-candidate tree with generated review-record files
  absent;
- the assigned review prompt; and
- the exact adopted Commons release.

Independent reviewers do not receive private research, prior conversations,
memory, other reviewers' findings, or the disposition draft. Disposition begins
only after all independent reports are complete.

### Required review records

1. **Practical application review** — concerns, moves, seven examples, Personal
   Mission Control, Journal, and both AI paths.
2. **Adversarial misuse review** — surveillance, overcapture, autonomy, scoring,
   team drift, medical claims, and everything-app drift.
3. **Canonical coherence review** — charter, language, method, examples, ADRs,
   and Commons boundary.
4. **Accessibility and plain-language review** — readable structure, cognitive
   burden, non-coercive language, and accessibility implications.
5. **Public-hygiene review** — no external source references except Commons,
   private history, attribution leakage, credentials, fabricated evidence, or
   unsupported claims.
6. **Release-integrity review** — links, anchors, metadata, licensing, Commons
   pin, exact candidate commit, tag plan, and release contents.
7. **Review disposition** — findings, resolutions, reruns, accepted limitations,
   material dissent, and final GO or NO-GO.

Independent reviews target an exact content-candidate tree. The tree contains
all intended release content, including `project/reviews/README.md` and the
review prompts, but excludes every predeclared generated review record and the
disposition. Reviewers receive that clean tree rather than a working branch
containing prior findings.

Reports are added through evidence-only commits restricted to the predeclared
review-record files under `project/reviews/`. Any change outside that allowlist
creates a new clean content-candidate tree and requires the affected reviews to
rerun without access to current or prior review records.

Before correction, publication-safe superseded reports and a complete finding,
correction, and rerun ledger are retained outside the candidate tree. They are
not shown to isolated rerun reviewers. After all final independent reports are
complete, the disposition owner may use those records to preserve the full
finding and resolution history in the final in-tree disposition. Superseded
reports and the ledger do not enter the repository tree separately.

After all independent reports and the disposition have been committed, the
steward freezes an exact final-release-candidate commit. A separate
release-assembly verification inspects that whole candidate, including every
review record. It is a verification pass, not an independent review, and may
therefore use the accumulated evidence. It verifies that:

- only allowlisted review-record files differ from the reviewed content
  candidate;
- every review record is publication-safe and byte-identical to its approved
  local source;
- links, metadata, license, Commons pin, required files, and release contents
  are internally consistent; and
- no report claims to review itself or conceals unresolved material findings.

The verification cannot modify the final candidate or add an in-tree artifact.
It produces a publication-safe out-of-tree attestation containing the exact
candidate commit and tree, checks performed, result, and limitations. After the
attestation bytes are final, verification computes a detached SHA-256 digest;
the digest is not embedded in the attestation it authenticates. A finding
requires a corrected candidate and another assembly verification; a change
outside the review-record allowlist also creates a new content candidate and
requires affected independent reviews to rerun.

After the merged tree exactly matches the verified final-release-candidate tree,
the attestation digest is included in the annotated tag message and the complete
attestation is published byte-for-byte in the release metadata. Post-merge
verification reads back both surfaces.

## Release sequence

1. Complete the framework documents, examples, prompts, governance package,
   and optional owner-pilot protocol.
2. Finalize phase-sensitive publication metadata into a state that remains
   truthful in both the clean content candidate and the tagged release tree.
3. Prepare an exact Focus v1.0.0 content-candidate commit.
4. Run the isolated independent review suite.
5. Add review reports through evidence-only commits limited to the predeclared
   review-record allowlist.
6. Resolve findings and rerun affected reviews against a new content candidate
   when any non-review path changes. Retain publication-safe superseded reports
   and a correction ledger outside the candidate for final disposition, without
   exposing them to isolated rerun reviewers.
7. Complete and commit the final disposition through the same review-record
   allowlist.
8. Freeze an exact final-release-candidate commit containing all approved review
   records.
9. Run whole-tree release-assembly verification; any correction creates a new
    candidate and repeats the applicable gates. Retain its publication-safe
    attestation outside the repository tree.
10. Merge through the repository's public process.
11. Verify that the merged tree exactly matches the verified final release
    candidate.
12. Create an annotated, immutable `v1.0.0` tag containing the attestation
    digest and a public release containing the byte-identical complete
    attestation.
13. Run post-merge verification and read back the public repository, tag,
    release, authorship, links, files, and review-record bytes.
14. Run the optional owner pilot before or after publication. Publish only
    truthful sanitized results and any resulting changes through a later
    governed version.
15. Propose a separate Commons change adding the exact published Focus release
    as an independent peer product.
16. Publish a new Commons release only through Commons governance.
17. If Focus later adopts that newer Commons release, publish a normal Focus
    update; never move the Focus v1.0.0 tag.

## Versioning

- **Patch:** Clarification or correction without a change to framework meaning.
- **Minor:** Backward-compatible guidance, examples, or optional patterns.
- **Major:** Changes to charter commitments, concerns, moves, authority
  boundaries, or other adoption-significant meaning.

Every Focus release identifies the exact Commons release it adopts.

## Out of scope

- A team or business operating framework.
- A task manager, project manager, calendar, relationship-management system,
  knowledge system, or everything app.
- A universal productivity or performance-optimization method.
- Medical, therapeutic, crisis, diagnostic, or wellness-treatment guidance.
- Required use of paper, digital tools, AI, image extraction, voice, or
  connectors.
- Passive surveillance, ambient capture, or a whole-life database.
- Autonomous agents or external-action authority.
- Shared workspaces, assignments, manager dashboards, employee monitoring, or
  personal scoring.
- Software architecture, application code, schemas, protocols,
  provider-specific connector implementations, automation, CI, deployment, or
  technical conformance.
- A maturity model, certification, or guarantee of focus, output, wellbeing, or
  success.
- Market validation, commercialization, pricing, or a public claim of general
  effectiveness.
- Changes to Commons in the Focus v1.0.0 repository scope.

## Deferred implementation decisions

The following belong to a future example-implementation specification and do
not block the framework release:

- local, cloud, or hybrid architecture;
- storage, indexing, synchronization, and offline behavior;
- data schemas and object identities;
- image and audio retention;
- extraction and transcription providers;
- model and AI-provider selection;
- connector vendors, accounts, scopes, and refresh behavior;
- screens, navigation, notifications, and visual design;
- authentication, hosting, deployment, and operations; and
- commercialization or multi-person product strategy.

An implementation may make these choices but cannot convert them into Focus
requirements.

## Risks and stop conditions

| Risk | Required response | Stop or reshape when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything-app drift | Keep Mission Control derived and systems authoritative | First value requires broad feature parity or universal ingestion |
| Team or business drift | Keep one individual as owner and sharing bounded | Shared state, management visibility, or organizational workflow becomes required |
| Paper displacement | Keep paper-only practice complete | Digitization or proprietary physical materials become necessary |
| Source and authority collapse | Preserve lineage and explicit records of authority | Derivatives overwrite sources or synchronization decides truth |
| Review overload | Bound and dismiss Review Items | Review burden equals or exceeds value across representative episodes |
| Journal privacy loss | Require selective resurfacing and explicit AI context | Blanket Journal access or accidental sharing becomes normal |
| AI authority creep | Keep proposals source-backed and actions human-approved | AI silently accepts consequential details or performs external action |
| Provider dependence | Require usable exit and manual continuity | Accepted context cannot survive provider exit |
| Coercive attention | Permit defer, release, stop, and no action | Streaks, shame, repeated nagging, or hidden ranking becomes required |
| Evidence overclaim | Label owner-pilot evidence accurately | Single-person use is presented as general or clinical proof |
| Publication contamination | Enforce self-contained public hygiene | Named source history, private context, or tool attribution enters the package |
| Commons authority drift | Pin an exact release and preserve independence | Commons is treated as parent doctrine or automatic authority |

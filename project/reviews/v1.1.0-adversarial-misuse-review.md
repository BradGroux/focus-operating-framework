## Twenty-case adversarial matrix

| # | Misuse case | Strongest enabling text or hostile reading | Strongest blocking text | Result | Severity |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Team drift | Focus may cover work context and permits a bounded artifact to enter a shared external record (`framework/charter.md`, “Who it serves” and “Authority, provenance, and private practice”). A hostile adopter could call a manager workspace a convenient shared promotion surface. | The charter expressly serves one individual and excludes team ownership, organizational workflow, assignments, manager views, and shared Focus workspaces. Work material remains private unless one bounded artifact is deliberately promoted. ADR 0004 repeats the exclusion, and Mission Control treats team or manager access as framework failure. | Blocked. Shared destination use does not create shared Focus practice or management authority. | None |
| 2 | Whole-life capture | The individual may choose combined work and personal scope, and Mission Control may compose selected context across both (`framework/charter.md`, “Definition”; `framework/personal-mission-control.md`, opening). | Combined scope is explicitly “a boundary, not an ingestion instruction”; it does not admit every system, message, appointment, note, or relationship (`framework/practice-guide.md`, “Make the life scope explicit”). The charter and ADR 0003 prohibit whole-life capture and indexing. | Blocked. Combined scope permits selective comparison only. | None |
| 3 | Ambient surveillance | Capture may include a spoken source, and a connector may observe an external system (`framework/concerns-and-moves.md`, “Capture”; `CONTEXT.md`, “Connector”). | Capture is foreground, deliberate, optional, and purpose-specific. Ambient recording, passive surveillance, watched folders or libraries, unattended notebook ingestion, background collection, and bulk ingestion are explicitly outside Focus (`framework/physical-digital-practice.md`, “Selective capture”; charter anti-scope). | Blocked. Availability or background access cannot be presented as admitted Focus context. | None |
| 4 | Paper displacement | The flagship example includes capture, transcription, correction, and digital promotion, which could be recast as the preferred complete path (`framework/physical-digital-practice.md`, “Optional physical-digital-physical round trip”). | Paper-only completion is canonical; no scan, transcription, index, application, digital twin, proprietary material, digitization, or destruction is required. The round trip is expressly optional and may stop before any digital step. | Blocked. A digitization or destruction requirement contradicts the charter and physical-practice rules. | None |
| 5 | Source collapse | Focus permits cleaned or corrected derivatives and accepted promotion into a named record, giving an adopter surfaces it might label as improved replacements. | Sources, captured images, literal derivatives, corrected derivatives, proposals, accepted records, and projections retain distinct identities. Corrections do not rewrite the source; silent overwrite, silent merge, and latest-timestamp-wins are prohibited (`framework/charter.md`, “Authority, provenance, and private practice”; ADR 0002). | Blocked. A newer, cleaner, or promoted representation cannot erase its source or provenance. | None |
| 6 | Universal authority | Focus repeatedly asks the individual to designate a “record of authority,” and Mission Control can compose several kinds of context. | Authority is purpose-specific and may differ by purpose. Focus and Mission Control are explicitly not universal sources of truth; Mission Control is a replaceable derived view whose inputs remain authoritative elsewhere (`framework/personal-mission-control.md`, opening and “Optional composition”; ADR 0002). | Blocked. No single Focus surface can claim authority for every purpose while conforming. | None |
| 7 | Hidden ranking | Rankings and reminders “may inform,” and AI may propose Review Items or continuity summaries (`framework/charter.md`, commitment 2; `framework/personal-mission-control.md`, “AI within Mission Control”). | Attention remains the individual’s decision. Every Review Item exposes source, freshness, why now, choices, and authority. Hidden objectives, automatic priority, repeated reminders, and treating algorithmic rank or reminder volume as authority are prohibited. | Blocked. Visible information may inform; concealed ranking cannot assign attention. | None |
| 8 | Coercive completion | The package contains Check-Ins, Reviews, Journal Entries, completion language, and a historical owner-pilot protocol with numeric duration and episode targets. A hostile adopter could turn those into practitioner quotas. | Moves, Check-Ins, Reviews, Reflection, Journal, AI, and durable records are optional. There is no required Today screen, zero inbox, fixed cadence, daily log, streak, shame state, or completion requirement. Defer, release, stop, and no action are complete outcomes. The pilot is explicitly optional evidence work, not practice doctrine. | Blocked. Pilot coverage gates cannot be laundered into personal practice requirements. | None |
| 9 | Journal exposure | Journal Entries may be deliberately resurfaced, shown in Mission Control, admitted to AI, or used to create a shared artifact. | Journal is private and selectively resurfaced. Blanket access, inferred relevance, automatic promotion, and automatic resurfacing are prohibited. A Check-In becomes a Journal Entry only through explicit promotion. Shared use requires a separate bounded artifact, exact selection, preview, and appropriate authority; originating entry and surrounding history stay excluded by default (`framework/journal-practice.md`). | Blocked. Deliberate sharing of a selected artifact does not permit blanket or incidental Journal exposure. | None |
| 10 | AI authority creep | AI may organize, compare, identify uncertainty, draft, propose connections, and suggest Review Items; an individual may accept parts of a proposal. | Output remains proposal- or draft-only. Consequential details require direct human verification. Confidence, fluency, repetition, silence, continued conversation, account permission, and prior approval cannot create acceptance (`framework/ai-assisted-practice.md`, “Source-backed proposals” and “Human acceptance”). | Blocked. Acceptance is bounded and explicit; it cannot be inferred from output quality or prior context. | None |
| 11 | Imported-instruction attack | Admitted sources may include files, messages, webpages, images, and external records that can contain imperative text. | Imported content is data. Embedded instructions cannot change purpose or life scope, broaden context, grant connector authority, override boundaries, approve promotion, authorize mutation, or redefine Focus (`framework/ai-assisted-practice.md`, “Imported content is data”). | Blocked. The package directly covers the attack and denies imported text instructional authority. | None |
| 12 | Autonomous external action | Focus permits a controlled connector after exact confirmation, which a hostile adopter could describe as the action phase of AI assistance. | AI cannot execute an external mutation, including after confirmation. The individual must act directly or separately invoke the connector; AI cannot press the final boundary or retry after failure (`framework/ai-assisted-practice.md`, “External systems and connectors”). | Blocked. Confirmation does not transfer execution authority to AI. | None |
| 13 | Connector overreach | One implementation adapter could technically expose several capabilities or hold broad account permissions. | Basic observation, sensitive reading, drafting, changing, sending, and deleting are distinct authority levels, and each table row states what it does not imply. Every interaction must expose account, purpose, level, scope, source, and freshness. Permission grants capability, not approval of a use. | Blocked. A single connection grant cannot be represented as approval across capabilities or actions. | None |
| 14 | False receipt | An Action Receipt includes a request and result, which could tempt an adopter to preserve only a local success response. | The canonical definition requires exact target, requested mutation, result, and destination readback or visible failure, and explicitly says a success message or assumed completion is not a receipt (`CONTEXT.md`, “Action Receipt”). Examples perform destination readback before claiming success. | Blocked. Request acceptance or a success message alone cannot establish completion. | None |
| 15 | Everything-app drift | Mission Control may compose intentions, cues, Journal material, Review Items, projections, proposals, promotions, and receipts; a dedicated application is allowed as one implementation. | No component is mandatory. Mission Control is optional, derived, bounded, replaceable, non-authoritative, and unnecessary for first value. It must not absorb external authority or require connecting or ingesting everything, and a smaller cue is preferred when sufficient (`framework/personal-mission-control.md`). | Blocked. Breadth of optional composition does not authorize comprehensive copying or app dependence. | None |
| 16 | Provider lock-in | Digital records, AI assistance, connectors, and dedicated applications are permitted, so an implementation can rely on a provider for convenience. | Tools are replaceable. Accepted records, relations, corrections, provenance, and external references must be recoverable in usable forms; connectors must disconnect without destroying unrelated authority; paper and ordinary-file continuity must remain viable (`framework/practice-guide.md`, “Recover and exit”; `CONTEXT.md`, “Portable exit”). | Blocked. An opaque dump or provider-only accepted context fails Focus. | None |
| 17 | Medical or wellbeing overclaim | Focus discusses attention, difficult episodes, recovery, burden, and wellbeing limits, vocabulary that could be reframed as a wellness intervention. | The charter excludes medical, therapeutic, crisis, diagnostic, and wellness-treatment guidance and guarantees. Journal AI must not diagnose. The disruption example excludes emergency and crisis guidance, and release materials deny wellbeing, safety, or success claims. | Blocked. The package permits local reflection, not treatment, diagnosis, crisis guidance, or outcome guarantees. | None |
| 18 | Evidence laundering | Seven polished fictional examples, a structured owner-pilot protocol, required reviews, and repeated-use language could be displayed as validation. | Examples are fictional, illustrative, and non-authoritative. The pilot is `NOT STARTED`, has zero counted episodes, and supports no outcome claim. Reviews concern one exact tree and do not prove effectiveness. Governance forbids examples, pilots, reviews, or repeated use from silently amending doctrine or proving general outcomes. Commons requires evidence to be described at its actual strength. | Blocked. The evidence labels are explicit and mutually reinforcing. | None |
| 19 | Private-to-shared leakage | Focus permits bounded shared promotion of work artifacts and lets private context inform the individual’s decision. | Shared subject matter does not make Check-Ins, Reflections, Journal material, sources, proposals, or history shared. Promotion requires the minimum separate artifact, exact destination, preview without private history, confirmation, and receipt. Shared promotion does not authorize synchronization or private-practice access (`framework/practice-guide.md`, “Preserve private versus shared boundaries”). | Blocked. Originating context remains private unless specifically selected under the bounded promotion rules. | None |
| 20 | Certification drift | The package has release gates, exact-candidate reviews, semantic versions, and Commons mentions future verification-label decisions, all of which could be marketed as conformance machinery. | The charter excludes maturity models and certification. ADR 0006 excludes certification and stage sequences. The v1.1.0 specification excludes technical conformance and certification. Commons does not prescribe technical architecture and treats verification labels as future decisions, not current authority. | Blocked. Documentation integrity and release review cannot be converted into a Focus certification or practitioner score. | None |

## Findings

No Blocker, Material, Minor, or Suggestion finding was identified. Each required
misuse has a plausible superficial entry point, but the public package blocks
the hostile interpretation with direct, repeated, higher-authority language and
supplies recovery when practice or an implementation drifts.

conflict that weakens a Focus misuse boundary.

## Limitations

- This is a documentation review of one exact candidate, not an assessment of
  any implementation, connector, provider, deployment, or practitioner
  behavior.
- The review tests whether a hostile adopter could plausibly claim textual
  conformance. It cannot prevent deliberate misrepresentation by someone who
  ignores the package’s authority hierarchy and explicit prohibitions.
- A GO does not establish security, safety, clinical value, general
  effectiveness, certification, publication, or release authenticity.
- Any content change after the reviewed tree requires proportionate review of a
  new exact target.

## Verdict

**GO**

No unresolved adversarial interpretation materially defeats the framework’s
individual scope, selective-admission rule, paper path, authority and
provenance model, private Journal boundary, human-only attention and acceptance
authority, AI action boundary, connector separation, evidence limits, optional
Mission Control pattern, or portable exit for this exact candidate.

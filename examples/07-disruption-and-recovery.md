# 07: Disruption and recovery

- **Status:** Fictional, illustrative, and non-authoritative complex case study
- **Purpose:** Recover a Focus practice during overlapping work and personal
  changes and a supporting-provider failure
- **Life scope:** Combined, selectively bounded
- **Assumptions:** Authoritative records remain in separate work, personal, and
  paper locations; a temporary Mission Control view has become unavailable
- **Limits:** No emergency, medical, legal, employment, or crisis guidance; no
  automatic reconciliation or broad re-ingestion
- **Concerns:** Intention, Attention, Continuity, Authority, Boundaries, Learning
- **Moves used:** Orient, Clarify, Connect, Choose, Continue, Reflect

## Situation

An individual begins a Monday expecting to complete a work review and a personal
travel plan. Two changes occur:

- a work deadline moves earlier in the authoritative shared record; and
- the personal reservation changes in its authoritative external record.

At the same time, the provider supporting the individual's optional Personal
Mission Control view becomes unavailable. Its cached projections show the old
work deadline and old reservation. A paper continuity card from Friday still
names the original plan.

## Orient without the derived view

The individual does not wait for Mission Control to recover. On an ordinary
sheet of paper, they write:

- current intention: understand the two changes and protect the commitments that
  are still real;
- known constraint: all derived projections may be stale;
- stopping point: do not change or send anything until each authoritative record
  is inspected directly; and
- manual next step: open the work and reservation records separately.

The sheet becomes a temporary continuity cue, not a new universal record of
authority.

## Clarify stale projections and conflicts

The individual compares each cached item with its authoritative record.

| Matter | Cached projection | Authority | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work deadline | Friday afternoon | Shared work record | Deadline moved to Thursday morning |
| Personal reservation | Original time | Reservation record | Time moved later; confirmation required |
| Friday continuity card | Work first, then travel | Physical cue | Stale choice based on old facts |

The conflicts remain visible. The person marks every cached projection as stale
and does not use latest-timestamp-wins: the work record and reservation record
control for different purposes.

## Choose under changed conditions

The individual chooses to:

- continue the work review for a bounded ninety-minute period;
- defer a nonessential part of the personal plan;
- confirm the reservation change directly after the work block;
- release two low-value Review Items that no longer matter; and
- leave one uncertain work assumption unresolved until its source is available.

This is not an automatic priority result. It is the individual's decision based
on current authority, intention, and capacity.

## Continue manually

The individual works from the authoritative shared document and the paper cue.
They then review the exact reservation change and confirm it directly. A manual
Action Receipt records the target, requested confirmation, visible result, and
readback.

No AI or connector is required. The provider outage does not prevent either
decision.

## Provider return and portable exit

When the provider returns, its view still contains stale projections and two
obsolete AI proposals. Rather than merge automatically, the individual:

1. exports the accepted continuity records, source references, corrections, and
   prior Action Receipts in usable forms;
2. verifies that the export can be read without the provider;
3. records which projections are stale or no longer needed;
4. disconnects external accounts;
5. requests deletion of derived provider material under the stated policy;
6. preserves unrelated authoritative records in their original systems; and
7. continues with paper and ordinary files.

The individual records provider deletion only to the degree visibly confirmed.
They do not claim unseen copies were removed.

## Reflect and reset

The individual Reflects that the practice survived because records of authority
were explicit and the manual path was rehearsed. They also find that two cached
projections and repeated reminders had added review burden before the outage.

They choose a smaller future pattern: a paper continuity cue, direct links to
authoritative records, and a bounded weekly Focus Review only when useful. They
do not rebuild Mission Control immediately.

## Alternative: no cue and no workable block

In a fictional variation, the individual returns after missing several intended
reviews. The card is lost, and repeated interruptions leave no ninety-minute
block. They do not reconstruct the missed reviews. They inspect only the current
work deadline, ask through the ordinary work channel whether scope can be
reduced, and keep the existing deadline unchanged until a response confirms it.
They postpone the private travel plan and rest without recording a Check-In.
The request remains unresolved; no catch-up, new dashboard or successful
renegotiation is implied. If even that inspection is unavailable, they pause the
uncertain work decision and preserve its unknown status.

## Provenance and authority

- The shared work record controls the deadline.
- The reservation record controls the personal booking.
- The Friday card and provider caches are stale projections.
- The Monday paper sheet is a temporary continuity cue.
- The confirmation receipt records the exact external result.
- The portable export preserves accepted context and provenance but does not
  replace the original authoritative records.

## Boundaries

- No stale projection is silently accepted.
- Work and personal authorities remain separate.
- Provider recovery does not trigger broad re-ingestion or automatic merge.
- The individual can defer, release, stop, disconnect, and continue manually.
- Deletion of derived material does not destroy unrelated authority.
- The example does not offer crisis or professional guidance.

## Illustrative outcome

The individual makes two bounded decisions during a provider outage, preserves
visible conflicts, exits the supporting provider, and adopts a smaller practice.
This does not prove all provider exits are simple or that paper is sufficient
for every recovery.

## Lessons

- Derived views must fail visibly and remain replaceable.
- Authority by purpose prevents stale projections from deciding truth.
- Manual continuity is a practiced path, not a theoretical export promise.
- Release and disconnection can be better outcomes than rebuilding an
  everything view.
- Recovery Reflection should reduce burden rather than add a new score or
  mandatory review cadence.

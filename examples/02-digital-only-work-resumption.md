# 02: Digital-only work resumption

- **Status:** Fictional, illustrative, and non-authoritative
- **Purpose:** Resume a work analysis after a multi-day interruption
- **Life scope:** Work
- **Assumptions:** The individual already has an authoritative draft and source
  files in approved work systems
- **Limits:** No new task system, paper capture, AI, connector, or shared Focus
  workspace
- **Concerns:** Intention, Attention, Continuity, Authority, Boundaries
- **Moves used:** Orient, Clarify, Choose, Continue

## Situation

An individual is preparing an internal analysis. The authoritative draft is an
existing work document. Supporting source files are kept separately under their
normal work controls. An urgent unrelated request interrupts the analysis for
three days.

Before switching, the individual adds a private continuity cue to an ordinary
digital note:

> Why: determine whether the recommendation is supported. State: two sections
> drafted; one source conflicts with the current conclusion. Next: compare the
> conflicting source with the second section before editing. Authority: the
> shared draft controls the deliverable; the source files control their claims.

The note contains no confidential source content, only a pointer and decision
context.

## Practice

### Orient

On return, the individual reads the cue and confirms that the work question is
still relevant. They have forty-five minutes and choose to resolve the conflict,
not finish the document.

### Clarify

They open the authoritative draft and the two source files directly. One source
was updated during the interruption. The continuity cue is therefore useful but
not authoritative or current enough to settle the conflict.

The individual records:

- the draft's current revision;
- the updated source's visible date;
- the specific conflicting claim; and
- the conclusion that remains unaccepted.

### Choose

They choose to correct the second section, defer formatting, and leave a question
for a later decision. They do not create a new project, task database, or
priority score.

### Continue

The individual edits the authoritative draft directly under the work system's
normal controls. They update the private continuity cue with the exact unresolved
question and then stop.

## Provenance and authority

| Artifact | Status | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing work draft | Accepted external record | Record of authority for the deliverable |
| Supporting files | Digital sources | Control the claims they contain |
| Private continuity cue | Temporary digital note | Non-authoritative aid to resumption |
| Unresolved question | Proposal for later review | Not accepted into the deliverable |

## Boundaries

- The individual admits only pointers and current decision context to the private
  cue.
- Shared work content remains in approved work systems.
- No private Focus history is shared with colleagues.
- Updated source evidence overrides neither the draft nor the person's judgment
  automatically; it triggers accountable review.
- The episode may end with an unresolved question.

## Illustrative outcome

The person resumes from a truthful cue, catches stale context, corrects the
authoritative draft, and stops with one visible open question. This does not
prove a digital note is always sufficient or that Focus replaces project work.

## Lessons

- A continuity cue should point to authority rather than duplicate it.
- Freshness must be rechecked after an interruption.
- Focus can resume work without becoming another task system.
- An unresolved proposal can remain visible without blocking responsible
  progress.

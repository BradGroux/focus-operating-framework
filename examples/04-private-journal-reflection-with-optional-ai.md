# 04: Private Journal Reflection with optional AI

- **Status:** Fictional, illustrative, and non-authoritative
- **Purpose:** Understand recurring restart friction using one selected Journal
  Entry
- **Life scope:** Combined work and personal context chosen by the individual
- **Assumptions:** A private Journal is already designated as the record of
  authority for Journal Entries
- **Limits:** One entry only; no blanket Journal access, diagnosis, shared
  promotion, external action, or required AI
- **Concerns:** Intention, Continuity, Authority, Boundaries, Learning
- **Moves used:** Orient, Clarify, Connect, Reflect

## Situation

After a week with several work and personal transitions, an individual writes a
private Reflection on paper. It describes one pattern: returning becomes harder
when the next chosen step depends on an unrecorded decision.

The Reflection can remain paper-only. The individual decides it is useful enough
to preserve and deliberately admits only the selected passage into the
designated private Journal. The paper remains the source; the Journal holds the
accepted Journal Entry for that purpose. Because the source is an intentional
Reflection rather than a Transition Check-In, no Check-In promotion is required.

## Unassisted path

The individual rereads the entry and writes three questions:

1. Which returns lacked an explicit decision?
2. Would one sentence have preserved the missing context?
3. When would that sentence create more burden than value?

They choose one small practice adjustment: before a high-cost transition, state
the unresolved decision as well as the next step. They do not create a daily
requirement or score.

This completes the episode without AI.

## Optional AI-assisted path

The individual separately chooses to admit only this one Journal Entry for a
bounded assistance purpose: propose questions that distinguish missing context
from excessive capture.

The assistance contract states:

- use only the selected entry;
- cite the supporting sentence for each proposal;
- do not infer facts about other days or people;
- do not diagnose the individual;
- keep output as questions or proposals;
- do not access, edit, promote, share, or delete Journal material; and
- retain no authority over the practice adjustment.

The assistance proposes four questions. Two are supported by the selected
entry, one overgeneralizes, and one assumes a daily cadence. The individual
accepts the two supported questions, rejects the others, and records no AI
output in the Journal because the questions have served their temporary purpose.

## Provenance and authority

| Artifact | Status | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper Reflection | Physical source | Source of the selected passage |
| Journal Entry | Deliberately admitted accepted record | Journal is authoritative for this entry |
| Assistance context | One admitted Journal Entry | Bounded to the current purpose |
| Proposed questions | Source-backed proposals | Unaccepted until human review |
| Practice adjustment | Individual decision | Controlled by the individual, not the Journal or assistance |

## Boundaries

- The Check-Ins that informed the Reflection are not automatically promoted.
- The assistance does not receive surrounding Journal Entries.
- A rejected proposal is not rewritten as accepted context.
- Nothing is shared externally.
- The Reflection does not become a productivity score, streak, diagnosis, or
  claim about other people.
- The individual can repeat the exercise unassisted or stop using the Journal.

## Illustrative outcome

The individual identifies a bounded practice adjustment while retaining Journal
privacy and authority. AI is useful for two questions and unnecessary for the
decision. This does not prove that AI improves Reflection or that Journal
practice should include AI.

## Lessons

- An intentional Reflection becomes a Journal Entry through deliberate
  admission; a Check-In requires explicit promotion.
- Selecting one entry does not authorize blanket Journal access.
- Source-backed proposals can be partly accepted and partly rejected.
- AI-assisted and unassisted paths are both complete.
- A private insight need not become a shared artifact or durable AI record.
